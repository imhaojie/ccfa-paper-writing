# Scientific writing and presentation

## Argument and structure

Build a coherent chain from the research problem and its boundary to a concrete failure, design requirement, method, controlled evidence, and implications. Establish a hook before introducing a new technical concept. Define nonstandard terminology, symbols and acronyms at their first meaningful use; use them consistently within each independently read document. Do not repeatedly expand acronyms for stylistic variety.

Give each paragraph a clear job: state its point, explain the reason or mechanism, provide decisive support, and bridge forward where useful. Organize sections by substantive research questions rather than fragmenting them into shallow lists. Distinguish established results from interpretations and future work.

## Style

Write natural, precise academic English with an explicit subject and clear logic. Minimize first-person authorial language when a natural alternative exists, but use it when it makes agency or decisions clearer. Favor claim-forward sentences and meaningful comparison over dense parentheses, long lists of numbers, or prose “VS.” Select headline results rather than reciting every cell. Avoid defensive wording; retain limitations relevant to validity, scope, ethics, deployment and reproducibility. Match causal language to the experimental design.

## Editing and layout

Preserve all authorized facts, figures, results, citations and locked sections. Prefer a useful local recast to filler when a line ends with one or two words or a page has avoidable whitespace. When the task includes PDF layout, render and visually inspect every affected page, including captions, figures, tables and neighboring pages after reflow; do not claim a visual check based only on source or logs. Keep captions self-contained and inspect large gaps. Use the venue's official rules as constraints; do not manipulate margins, fonts or spacing to evade them. Natural page endings and scientific clarity take precedence over mechanically filling every line or page.

When a full submission audit is requested, verify sources, equations, symbols, references and figure/table links within the available evidence. State unchecked items explicitly; do not equate successful compilation with scientific verification.
