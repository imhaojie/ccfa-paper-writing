# Research review and rebuttal

## Review

Read the actual manuscript, supporting materials and authoritative results, noting which version was submitted. Reconstruct the central claim and problem boundary before critiquing. Assess novelty against the closest verified prior work, soundness, experiments, baselines, statistics, clarity, limitations, and significance. Report concrete evidence and locations, severity, why each issue matters, and the smallest useful remedy. Distinguish factual error, unsupported interpretation, missing evidence, unclear presentation, and reviewer misunderstanding. Avoid invented scores, guaranteed acceptance, or treating a model's assessment as peer review.

## Rebuttal preparation

Collect every review, score and confidence if provided, response limits, deadline, venue rules, and whether new results or attachments are permitted. Read the frozen submitted paper, supplement, and results before drafting. If a policy or detail is unavailable, identify the gap rather than inventing it.

Build a concern-to-evidence matrix: reviewer and original concern | underlying question | severity | submitted evidence and precise location | validity or uncertainty | response strategy | minimum additional test, cost and permitted status. Merge related concerns without losing reviewer-specific questions. Prioritize by potential effect on the decision, severity, strength of existing evidence, and time or resource cost. Separate completed results, analyses possible with existing data, proposed experiments, and unresolvable uncertainty.

## Response

Answer the actual concern directly. Give the conclusion, strongest verified evidence and its relevance, then state the necessary boundary or limitation. Use a brief, professional acknowledgment; do not accuse reviewers of missing content, rely on repetitive thanks, or present speculative new results as completed. Engage valid criticism candidly, correct misunderstandings with exact references, and distinguish what the submission already established from what can be added later. Do not promise changes or experiments the authors have not authorized.

Adapt format and length to the official response interface. Check every number, citation, table and page against the submitted artifacts; reconcile shared claims across reviewers. Count the actual output using the platform's relevant rule if known, identify uncovered questions, and audit for overclaiming and contradiction. If final limits are not provided, supply a usable draft with the assumption stated.
