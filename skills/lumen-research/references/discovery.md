# Discovery: literature, ideas, and novelty

## Literature

Frame the research question and terminology; inspect relevant user-provided documents and local literature first. Search current external sources when the task requires current coverage or verification. Use complementary terminology, method names, adjacent formulations, and backward and forward citation trails. Prioritize primary sources, including official proceedings and the original paper. Record search date, scope, and access limitations.

For each relevant paper, record title, authors, year, venue or preprint status, primary link, problem, assumptions, mechanism, evaluation, principal finding, and relation to the user's work. Check details in the paper rather than asserting claims from a search snippet. Group papers by research question or approach, synthesize consensus and disagreements, and distinguish gaps in the literature from gaps in the search.

## Generate and assess ideas

Express each promising idea as: problem and why it matters; testable hypothesis; mechanism; closest prior work and a concrete difference; minimum experiment; resource needs; plausible negative result; and risks. Compare research value, novelty evidence, feasibility, and cost against the user's goals. Identify assumptions and early falsification tests. Avoid presenting speculative mechanisms as established facts.

## Novelty

Decompose the proposed contribution into problem, setting, mechanism, evaluation and empirical finding. Search separately for technical overlap and task overlap, using multiple formulations and recent as well as foundational work. Compare the strongest neighboring sources side by side: shared elements, meaningful difference, experimental conditions, and what would distinguish the contributions. State uncertainty and coverage. A search cannot prove absolute novelty; do not use numerical novelty scores as a substitute for evidence.

If a pilot could resolve the highest-risk assumption, propose its design and budget. Execute only when within the user's actual task and authorized resources. Do not commit to a GPU budget, time limit, or external reviewer without evidence that it is available and appropriate.
